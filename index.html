The big ideas

Factory360's challenge isn't finding clients — it's having enough hands when great opportunities show up. Everything in this plan builds on that.

Build a flexible freelance team

Hire one experienced full-time Freelance Manager to recruit, organize and lead a ready-to-go roster for last-minute projects.

AI training that fits each role

Expand the executive AI budget to every department — tailored by position, with clear security rules and no wasted subscriptions.

Get help with hiring

Add a part-time HR specialist (ideally from events) to screen, verify and onboard — so your time goes back to clients.

Make great feedback even better

Keep your fast, specific feedback. Add 15-minute project wrap-ups, an anonymous survey, and more praise for wins.

Rewards people choose

A yearly rewards budget where employees pick what matters to them, plus Employee of the Quarter and Year.

Stay ahead on freelancer law

Your PEO and payroll approach is working. Keep it, and keep an updated state-by-state compliance guide as you grow.

HR strategy · Recruitment · Retention

People & staffing

The heart of our plan: the right people available at the right time — without the cost of hiring a lot of full-time staff.

New role · Full-time

Freelance Manager

An experienced person who knows experiential marketing and can work independently. They would:

  • Recruit and interview freelancers
  • Keep an up-to-date roster of who's available and their skills
  • Match freelancers to projects
  • Oversee the work so it meets Factory360's standards

Why it matters: when a rush project lands, qualified people are already lined up — so you say "yes" more often, protect margins, and take pressure off your current team.

New role · Part-time

HR Specialist

Someone (ideally from the events industry) to help with interviewing, background checks/verification, and onboarding — freeing up the hours you currently spend interviewing.

Heads-up: experienced people often prefer full-time work, so this role needs to be "sold." Lead with your great culture, flexibility where possible, room to grow, and benefits like medical, dental and 401(k) for eligible employees.

Final hiring decisions stay with you and your managers — culture fit is too important to outsource.

Who to hire: the Factory360 profile

⚡

Timely & responsive

Deadlines and last-minute changes are part of the job.

🤝

Loyal

A track record of staying in roles — not leaving after a year or two.

😊

Kind & respectful

Nice people who get along and keep the atmosphere great.

💬

Great communicators

Projects cross departments, so clear communication keeps them smooth.

Improve

Make hiring easier

  • Standard interview questions and a scoring guide (background, experience, critical thinking).
  • Look for people who bring new ideas and perspectives, not just people who "fit in."
  • Approved AI tools can help prepare the process — people make the decisions.
  • Clear onboarding on mission and values so new hires know how they contribute.
Improve

Help people grow (and stay)

  • Training beyond AI: communication, project management, new tech.
  • Cross-department knowledge sharing so everyone understands how the whole company works.
  • Treat freelancers by their real working arrangement — not all the same way.

Training · Technology · Security

AI & technology, the Factory360 way

You've already started by giving the executive team an AI course budget. We recommend growing that into a company-wide plan — tailored, safe and cost-smart.

🔍

1. Map each role

Look at every position: which tasks AI could improve, and which skills each person actually needs.

🎯

2. Train by department

Creatives use AI to research and brainstorm while keeping ideas original. Project teams automate admin.

👩‍🏫

3. Pick great teachers

Instructors who know AI and the events industry. Training days where people learn, then teach their teams.

🔄

4. Share monthly

A monthly tech & AI trends meeting where colleagues demo what's working.

Protect

Clear AI rules, before connecting anything

You raised cybersecurity as a real concern — we agree. Create simple guidelines on:

  • Which AI tools are approved
  • What client and company information can (and can't) be shared
  • Security checks before connecting AI to email, spreadsheets or business apps

Everyone completes guideline training before using AI with sensitive information.

Save money

Test before you buy

Not everyone needs the same tools, and subscriptions add up. Check each tool for cost, security, whether it actually works, and impact on quality before rolling it out.

Great example already in motion: automating the collection of potential-client info that used to be done by hand.

Golden rule: AI supports people — it never replaces their creativity, judgment or client relationships.

Three tools worth a look

Ashby
Recruiting platform

Organizes applications, tracks candidates and schedules interviews — helpful now that some roles get hundreds of applicants instead of ~10.

LASSO
Event workforce

Built for managing event workers and schedules. Lets the Freelance Manager see who's available for upcoming events at a glance.

Granola
AI meeting notes

Captures and organizes meeting notes so client details don't get lost between back-to-back meetings.

No extra hires needed to run these — the recruiter, Freelance Manager and current managers can own them. You'd mainly need a subscription budget and some training time.

04
Performance appraisals · Communication

Feedback & performance

Good news: you're already doing what we planned to recommend — fast, specific, factual feedback ("you missed a deadline"). So our goal is to make it even stronger, not to change it.

✗

What we're NOT recommending: monthly one-on-ones. With two managers (Creative and Accounts) each leading about 24 people, that would overload already-packed schedules.

Keep

Immediate, specific feedback

Fix problems while they're small. It fits your values of transparency and honesty perfectly.

Add

Kick-off expectations

Before each project, managers share the process and what "great" looks like — fewer surprises later.

Add

15-minute wrap-ups

After each project (or weekly), a quick chat folded into existing meetings: what went right, what went wrong, what to do better.

Add

Feedback goes both ways

Invite honest feedback about the company, the team and managers — a safe space when something isn't working.

Add

Anonymous survey or suggestion box

For concerns people won't raise face-to-face. Helps everyone feel heard and valued.

Grow

Clear expectations & simple data

Set standards for communication, deadlines, teamwork and client satisfaction. Track workloads, response times and freelancer performance to spot issues early.

The missing piece

Celebrate the wins, too 🎉

Most feedback today is corrective. Recognizing creativity, teamwork, deadlines met and great client service reinforces exactly the behavior you want more of.

Benefits & rewards

  • Finder's fee for new business
  • Salaries $50K – $350K+
"Compensation strategies vary depending on the person."— Walter Burton, retired marketing entrepreneur. Some people want time off, some want commission, some just want to be recognized.
Main recommendation

A rewards budget where employees choose 🎁

Set aside a yearly budget for rewards — and let people pick. No more pizza Fridays. Options could include:

  • An extra paid day off
  • A certification or course they're excited about
  • A yearly team trip for the hardest-working team

Employee of the Quarter picks their reward. Employee of the Year gets something bigger — a bonus, a trophy, or a benefit of their choice.

New

👶 Paternity leave

Two paid weeks for new dads (an idea from John Cejka, VP of Operations at Air Esscentials) — add or expand if not already in your policy.

New

🎂 Paid birthday

An extra day off during your birthday month. Small gesture, big heart.

New

🏫 Family flexibility

Room for parents to attend school activities and important family events.

Compliance · Freelancers · Hiring law

Legal & compliance

Bottom line: you're on the right path. Running freelancers through payroll and full-time staff through a PEO already avoids most pitfalls. As the freelance team grows, a few rules deserve extra attention.

Know the rules

Who counts as a freelancer?

Federally, the Department of Labor uses the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). States add their own tests:

  • 27 states use the full "ABC test": the worker is (A) free from your control, (B) doing work outside your usual business, and (C) running their own independent business.
  • 6 states drop part B.
  • New York is one of 18 states using a "common-law control test" — it looks at who directs the work.
New York

"Freelance Isn't Free Act"

NYC passed it in 2017 to make sure freelancers get paid on time. In 2024, New York State expanded it statewide (General Business Law, Article 44-A) with written-contract requirements and formal enforcement.

It's taken seriously: the Attorney General can seek up to $25,000 in civil penalties for repeated violations, and NYC recovered $500,000+ from Splashlight, a local creative production company, for paying freelancers late.

What to do

⏩

Speed up the Freelance Manager

Rush jobs are when mistakes happen. A dedicated owner keeps contracts and payments clean — or use a third-party freelancer platform to limit liability.

💲

Salary ranges in job posts

New York requires them. Other states have their own rules.

📒

One living compliance guide

A shared document of each state's hiring and freelancer rules, kept up to date — with New York first.

Budget · HR outsourcing · Economy

Budget & outsourcing

Spend where it creates capacity; save where it doesn't.

Keep

Your PEO

Keep outsourcing payroll, benefits and HR compliance. It cuts admin work, brings in expertise, and keeps leadership focused on clients and growth. A full internal HR department doesn't make sense at Factory360's size.

Keep in-house

Hiring decisions

Interviews and final decisions stay with people who know the culture. You value responsible, hard-working, respectful team players — that judgment can't be outsourced.

Smarter spend

Freelancers over extra full-timers

Full-time hires mean salary, benefits, insurance and training — even in slow months. Freelancers scale up and down with the work.

Smarter spend

AI by need, not by default

Check what each department needs before buying subscriptions or courses. Same productivity, lower bill.

🌦

Ready for any economy. Inflation, high labor costs or clients cutting marketing budgets can change how many projects come in. A flexible team lets Factory360 adjust its workforce to match.

Satisfaction · Competitive edge · Global view

Culture & competitive edge

Your biggest advantage is the one nobody can copy: your people and your culture.

🏆

Why Factory360 wins

  • All services under one roof — idea to results
  • Experience with big names (Amazon, Meta and more)
  • Focus on measurable results, not just pretty events
  • A team that loves working together
  • Soon: a freelance bench to say "yes" faster than competitors

Competitors can copy meetings, AI tools or a freelance roster. They can't copy your experience, relationships, creativity and culture.

🌱

Keeping employees happy

  • Shared training across departments — builds understanding and connection
  • Monthly AI & tech meetups — learn, share ideas, bring new insights
  • Short project reviews — people feel heard; problems get caught early
  • Freelance support — less pressure on a team at full capacity

None of this changes the culture — it amplifies what's already working.

🌎

Cross-cultural awareness

Not every idea lands the same way everywhere. A campaign that works for one audience could flop — or offend — another because of beliefs and traditions. Open-minded, respectful teams (and cross-department idea sharing) keep experiences memorable for the right reasons.

🗺

Geopolitical & legal watch

World events rarely touch day-to-day work, but trade policy, economic shifts or conflicts can change global clients' marketing budgets. Employment and salary laws also differ by state — stay informed as the freelance team grows.
